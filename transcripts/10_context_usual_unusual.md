# MMC Video 10 — You need Context, but what is Context?

**Series:** Money Making Concepts (MMC) by Arjo Janssens
**Topic:** Usual & Unusual Context (context areas)
**Duration:** 22:41

---

## Summary
- **Context = the area where we look for an entry.** Defined as: from a **narrative area (boundary)** to the **first opposing PD array (target)**. Both are mechanical/objective.
  - The narrative area (FVG/FVA/swing point) is the **boundary** — as soon as price trades into it, drop to the lower timeframe to look for entry confirmation.
  - The **target** = first opposing PD array (if expecting higher from a discount array, the target is the nearest premium array, e.g. a swing high).
- **We always trade USUAL context** — even when the bigger picture is unusual context. Data shows context makes trades higher probability (it's the *last* area price can continue from).
- **Context areas by defense type:**
  - **FLOD (FVG):** FVG → swing high (or → previous candle high if no swing high).
  - **ODD (FVA):** FVA → swing point (or → previous candle high).
  - **LOD (swing point), two forms:** (1) swing low (no FVG) → swing high; (2) previous-candle-low sweep → first opposing PD array.
- After reaching a context target, **stop looking for entries** until new context forms — context highs/lows are often swept to continue.
- **Unusual context:** market is either **offering fair value** OR **seeking liquidity**. When an FVA's rejection fails and a bearish FVG forms instead → no longer offering fair value → seeking liquidity (target = FVA low). That bearish FVG is the **usual context** you trade, with the FVA low as the external target.
  - If the FVA overlaps an FVG → expect a clean rejection. If the FVA is being **swept** (no overlapping FVG) → already seeking liquidity → trade the follow-up bearish FVG toward the FVA low.
- **Examples used:** CAD/JPY, USD/CHF, USD/JPY, GBP/AUD, gold, crude oil.
- **Case study:** study context areas and see where potential entries form (hindsight backtesting on the lower timeframe).
