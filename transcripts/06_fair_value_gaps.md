# MMC Video 06 — Everything you need to know about Fair Value Gaps

**Series:** Money Making Concepts (MMC) by Arjo Janssens
**Topic:** Good vs bad Fair Value Gaps (FVG types)
**Duration:** 24:06

---

## Summary
- Not every FVG is equal. The **first line of defense** is ideally an FVG — but within that, FVGs differ by quality.
- **Target on the left, trade from the right** (most recent order flow lag / FVG).
- **FVGs get created at unmitigated opposing PD arrays** (resistance/support that hasn't been traded into yet). "Unmitigated" = not yet traded into; the smallest touch mitigates it.
- An FVG is a **3-candle pattern**: candle 1 (anything), candle 2 = expansion-phase candle (closes beyond candle 1's high/low, defining the potential gap), candle 3 determines the **type**:
  - **Rejection FVG (RFVG):** large rejection 3rd candle → too much opposing strength → **worst** FVG to trade from. Often comes off a **sweep** of an unmitigated opposing PD array. Avoid, or demand much more confirmation.
  - **Perfect FVG (P-FVG):** consolidation 3rd candle → enough opposing strength for a retracement but not a full rejection → **best** FVG to trade from (~80/20 balance). Easiest on any timeframe; focus here when starting out.
  - **Breakaway gap (BWG):** expansion 3rd candle → ~100% one-sided strength, no retracement → not worst/not best; harder to enter/manage. Only trade when the unmitigated opposing PD array (target) has **not** been reached yet; capitalize by dropping a timeframe to find the order flow lag that delivers to the target.
- **Case study:** mark all FVGs on any timeframe, see which hold and which don't, and label them (RFVG / P-FVG / BWG).
