## Summary

- Still focused on **finding direction**. *Price is always moving towards a PD Array* — our job is to find which one is next.
- To know which PD Array is next, we need the **Fair Value Area (FVA)**. To understand FVA, we first need **Market Structure**.

## Market Structure — Intermediate Term Highs/Lows (ITH / ITL)
- **Intermediate Term High (ITH):** a swing high with a **lower swing high to the left and to the right**.
- **Intermediate Term Low (ITL):** a swing low with a **higher swing low to the left and to the right** (inverse of ITH).
- **In a bearish trend:** ITHs are **protected** (price does not trade back above them); ITLs get traded below.
- **In a bullish trend:** ITLs are **protected** (price does not trade below them); ITHs get traded above.
- If price violates the expected protection (e.g. trades back above an ITH in a downtrend), the *other* side's level becomes the one to respect → early sign of a flip.

## Timeframe workflow
1. Mark **swing points + fair value gaps** on the **daily** timeframe.
2. Drop to **4H** (and **1H** if needed) to spot **ITHs / ITLs**.
3. The lower-TF market structure tells the story of which **daily PD Array** price wants to reach next.
- If 4H already shows clear ITHs/ITLs, no need to go to 1H.

## Fair Value Area (FVA) — definition & theory
- An FVA forms from a **three-swing movement** (e.g. ITL → ITH → continuation), leaving an area **from the ITH to the ITL**.
- Two sources of an FVA:
  1. **Market structure:** from ITH → ITL and vice versa.
  2. **Order flow:** from short-term high → short-term low and vice versa (covered in Video 03).
- **Theory — the market likes to offer fair value:** every participant deserves a fair chance to get involved.
  - A single fast swing (only sellers got involved) hasn't offered fair value → price **retraces** to offer buyers and new sellers a fair chance.
  - Within an area that has *already* offered fair value, there's no reason to revisit deeply.
  - After breaking an ITL, the new "unfair" area is **below that ITL** → the ideal deepest retracement is back to the **start of the FVA (the ITL)**, then continuation lower.
- **Practical use:** when FVAs keep holding and ITLs keep getting traded below → safe to assume continuation lower toward targets (and inverse for bullish).
- If a rejection at an FVA fails to follow through, that's an **early warning** the trend may be flipping (fair value no longer being offered).

## Case study (Video 02 homework)
- Mark swing points + fair value gaps on a **daily** chart (any market: EURGBP, GBPUSD, NASDAQ, Bank Nifty, crypto).
- Drop to **4H / 1H**, read the market structure (ITHs/ITLs), and try to predict which daily PD Array price will reach in the coming days.
